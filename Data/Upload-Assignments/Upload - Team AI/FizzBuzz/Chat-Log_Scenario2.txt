SCENARIO 2 - GLOBAL TEAM, SHIFTING REQUIREMENTS

THE STORY

LearnLoop is a startup that just raised 3.2M EUR to build an AI-powered adaptive learning platform for universities. 12 people, three cities, very different skill sets:

Amsterdam (UTC+1) – 4 people:

Product managers, UX designers, 1 frontend developer

Strength: user research, product vision, GDPR knowledge

Weakness: no ML or backend expertise

Bangalore (UTC+5:30) - 5 people:

Backend engineers, ML engineers, 1 DevOps engineer

Strength: recommendation systems, scalable infrastructure

Weakness: no direct contact with end users or customers

Austin (UTC-6) - 3 people:

Business development, data analyst, customer success

Strength: weekly calls with the pilot customer (University of Texas)

Weakness: no technical or product background

Timezone reality:

Amsterdam ↔ Bangalore: 4.5h gap - small morning overlap only

Amsterdam ↔ Austin: 7h gap - small evening overlap only

Bangalore ↔ Austin: 11.5h gap - nearly zero overlap

The first pilot customer wants a working MVP (Minimum Viable Product) in 9 months.

After the first stakeholder workshops, three problems surface immediately:

Nobody at the university agrees on what the platform should do.

IT wants LMS integration. Professors want content control.

	Students want to see why a recommendation was made.  
Legal wants FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act)

compliance before any student data is processed.

Austin collects new requirements every week in customer calls,

but there is no process to pass them to Amsterdam or Bangalore.

Bangalore has already started building the recommendation engine -

	but Amsterdam has not yet decided what data the platform will collect. 

	Two engineers are frustrated because they keep receiving design mockups 

	that are architecturally impossible to implement. 
The CEO is optimistic. The team is not.

SITUATION A - WHOSE DESIGN WINS?

Amsterdam has designed a user interface that requires real-time

personalisation data streamed from the backend.

Bangalore says this is technically impossible with the current

architecture - a full redesign would take 3 months.

You have 9 months until the MVP deadline.

You cannot do a 3-month redesign AND build all features in time.

You have three options:

(1) Amsterdam adapts the design to fit the current architecture. 

    Risk: the user experience becomes significantly worse. 

(2) Bangalore does the redesign. Sprint 1-6 produce almost nothing 

    visible, putting the 9-month deadline at serious risk. 

(3) Ship the MVP with the weaker UX (option 1), then redesign 

    after the pilot customer has seen it. 
Which do you choose?

Who has the authority to make this call - and what happens if

Amsterdam and Bangalore cannot agree?

SITUATION B - THE ONLY MEETING SLOT

You have exactly one 90-minute slot per week where all three teams

can meet synchronously (15:00-16:30 Amsterdam time).

This week you have three agenda items that are all critical:

(1) Finalise the data model - Bangalore is blocked without this. 

(2) Review 6 new requirements from the university - Austin needs 

    a decision before the next customer call on Friday. 

(3) Resolve the Amsterdam/Bangalore architecture conflict 

    from Situation A - it has been unresolved for 2 weeks. 
You can cover two of the three properly in 90 minutes.

Which item do you drop from the live meeting?

How do you handle it differently so it does not stall the team?

SITUATION C - THE LEGAL BLOCKER

Halfway through Sprint 3, Austin calls with urgent news:

the University of Texas legal team has blocked all student data

collection until a FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act)

compliance agreement is signed. Signing takes 6-8 weeks minimum.

Bangalore is currently mid-sprint, building the recommendation engine

that depends entirely on that student data.

You have three options:

(1) Stop Bangalore's work immediately. Wait 6-8 weeks. 

    Cost: 6-8 weeks of lost development time and team frustration. 

(2) Continue building the engine on synthetic (fake) data. 

    Risk: the engine may need significant rework once real data arrives 

    and behaves differently than expected. 

(3) Redirect Bangalore to a different part of the product 

    (e.g., the LMS integration) while legal is resolved. 

    Risk: context switching costs and the engine falls further behind. 
Which option do you choose?

How do you communicate this decision to the Bangalore team?

TASK 2 - DECIDE
SITUATION A - WHOSE DESIGN WINS?

I would choose option (3): Ship the MVP with the weaker UX, then redesign after the pilot customer has seen it. The reasoning behind this choice is that delivering a functional MVP is crucial to gather real user feedback, which can be invaluable for future iterations. Additionally, the risks associated with the other two options could jeopardize the MVP deadline, ultimately risking the project.

The authority to make this call likely rests with the project manager or product owner, as they have to balance time constraints with user needs. If there’s a disagreement between Amsterdam and Bangalore, facilitating a clear dialogue, possibly with a structured decision-making framework, could help resolve the issue while ensuring all teams are aligned on the MVP’s goals.

SITUATION B - THE ONLY MEETING SLOT

I would drop agenda item (3) related to resolving the Amsterdam/Bangalore architecture conflict. This conflict has already stalled for two weeks, and focusing on finalizing the data model and reviewing new requirements could prevent further blockers.

To handle the dropped item differently, I would suggest setting up a separate asynchronous dialogue, such as a dedicated Trello board or Slack channel, where team members from both Amsterdam and Bangalore can discuss and propose solutions at their convenience without delaying other urgent matters.

SITUATION C - THE LEGAL BLOCKER

I would choose option (3): Redirect Bangalore to a different part of the product (e.g., the LMS integration) while legal is resolved. This option allows the team to keep making progress on the product and not lose momentum while waiting for legal approval.

When communicating this decision to the Bangalore team, I would ensure to explain the context of the legal blockage, emphasizing that this is a temporary redirection and that their work on the recommendation engine will resume as soon as possible. I'd also encourage them to share any concerns they have about context switching or workload.
